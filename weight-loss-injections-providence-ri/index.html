    How Weight Loss Injections Work in Providence, RI

    How Weight Loss Injections Work in Providence

    Most modern weight loss shots are GLP-1 medicines. They copy a hormone your gut releases after eating, which slows stomach emptying, signals fullness to the brain and helps regulate blood sugar after a meal. Semaglutide acts on GLP-1 alone. Tirzepatide acts on both GLP-1 and GIP receptors, which may explain its larger average effect in trials.

    Who Weight Loss Injections Are For

    FDA-approved injections are for adults with a BMI of 30 or higher. Adults with a BMI of 27 or more also qualify if they have a weight-related condition such as high blood pressure, type 2 diabetes, high cholesterol or sleep apnea. Diet and activity still count. They are not for pregnancy or certain thyroid cancer histories.

    Injection Dosing Schedule in Providence

    Every weekly injection starts low. Wegovy begins at 0.25 mg and climbs every four weeks through 0.5, 1 and 1.7 mg to a maintenance dose of 2.4 mg, though some people stay at 1.7 mg. Zepbound starts at 2.5 mg and rises in 2.5 mg steps to as high as 15 mg. Same day each week.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    Appetite often drops in the first week. The first month tends to show small changes on the scale since the dose is low, while stomach upset clusters right after each shot. In the STEP 1 trial, adults on semaglutide 2.4 mg lost about 15 percent of body weight on average over 68 weeks, and SURMOUNT-1 reported about 20 percent with tirzepatide 15 mg over 72 weeks. Results vary.

    Injection Side Effects in Providence

    Nausea is the classic one. Diarrhea, constipation, vomiting, heartburn and mild redness at the injection site are also common, especially in the days after starting or raising the dose. Smaller meals, protein first and plenty of water help most people. Less common but serious problems include pancreatitis, gallstones and kidney strain from dehydration.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Screening starts with BMI and medical history. Your provider then reviews every medication you take, looking hardest at insulin, sulfonylureas and birth control pills, since tirzepatide can make oral contraceptives less reliable after dose changes. A family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2 is a hard stop. Pregnancy and breastfeeding are too.

    Brand-Name vs Compounded Injections in Providence

    Wegovy and Zepbound are FDA-approved for weight management. Ozempic and Mounjaro contain the same active ingredients but are FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes, not weight loss alone. Compounded semaglutide and compounded tirzepatide are prepared by licensed compounding pharmacies and are not FDA-approved, so they have not gone through the same review for safety, effectiveness and manufacturing quality. Availability drives some people toward them.
